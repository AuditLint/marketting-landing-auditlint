The problem

Every team has rules. Nobody has time to check them.

Picture a studio of eight architects. Leads live in the CRM, plans and drawings live on a file share. The naming convention is in a document somewhere. Six months later, the two systems disagree in hundreds of small ways, and keeping everyone in line has become a manager's full-time worry.

Names spelled differently

The same client is written three ways across three tools, so search and reporting quietly break.

Fabrikam≠Fabricam Inc

Records missing on one side

A deal is won in the CRM, but nobody created the project folder. Or the folder exists for a deal that was lost.

crm: Datum Corp.→no folder

Files in the wrong place

Contracts saved to the root, drafts next to finals, documents nobody can find when the auditor asks.

/margies-contract.pdf?
Approach

Measure. Act. Monitor.

We write your conventions down as precise, testable rules and run them against your real systems. The first report is usually sobering. After the clean-up, AuditLint stays on and catches new drift the morning it appears.

IMeasure

Connect your tools read-only and run your rules across everything. You see every inconsistency on day one, counted and listed.

IIAct

Findings come as row-level lists your team can work through. Known exceptions are recorded once and stop showing up.

IIIMonitor

Scheduled runs keep checking. When something slips, the person who made the change hears about it while it's still fresh.

What changes

Less mental load. Fewer awkward conversations.

Most of the value is felt by the people who used to do the checking.

  • Managers stop policing

    The rules are checked for you, every night, across every record. You look at a short list instead of keeping the whole picture in your head.

  • Feedback without friction

    A colleague hears about a misnamed folder from a report, not from their manager. The tone stays neutral and nobody takes it personally.

  • New hires learn on the job

    Conventions are learned by doing. A new employee's first mistakes show up the next morning with the rule that explains them.
